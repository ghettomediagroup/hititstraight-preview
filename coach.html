From caddie at 12 to coach

How golf found Dion, and why he stayed with it

Dion lives in Woodlawn, on the South Side of Chicago. He was 12 years old when he started caddying. The game got hold of him early, and by his own account it pulled him out of trouble he could have found instead.

He kept playing and he kept learning, and about 20 years later he's still doing both. People told him he had the talent to make it as a pro. He has a wife and a child, and touring would have taken him away from them, so he chose to teach instead.

His process with every student is the same. He finds out what the student actually wants to learn, sets goals around it, and moves forward from there. He works with students of all ages.

Questions parents and students ask

These answers are Dion's own, reproduced from the academy's current website.

  • What is your typical process for working with a student? First and foremost, I want to figure out exactly what the student wants to learn. Then, we set goals to accomplish the desired outcome, and move forward from there.
  • What education and/or training do you have that relates to your work? I am a certified club fitter, certified club smith and I have 20 years experience playing and teaching the sport.
  • How did you get started teaching? People always told me I had the talent to make it as a golf pro. I have a wife and a child, and I knew touring would take time away from them, so I decided to go the golf instructor route.
  • What types of students have you worked with? I have worked with students as young as 2 years of age, all the way up to Division 1 college golfers aspiring to reach the professional tour.
  • Share one of your many success stories. One of my fondest memories is being a part of helping two students earn Division 1 college scholarships.
  • What advice would you give a student looking to hire a golf instructor? Interview wisely. Speak with a prospective golf instructor and ask questions to learn their knowledge of the game. It is also important that they be able to translate that knowledge in a way you understand.
